# Stationed fleet preparation

Fleets opens from a controlled system's inspector. Its illustrated instrument reserves local fighters and infantry, displays accepted execution ticks, and retains the draft through pending/reconnect/error states. At settlement, the manifest lists the actual fleet and a private outcome. Disbanding returns its forces at a subsequent tick. The galaxy shows the owner's real stationed-fleet count, and Back restores focus to the opening control. Phone keeps a readable image header, bounded manifest and safe-area commitment rail; desktop dedicates the scene to the ship with the manifest alongside it.

This slice implements formation and disbanding at a stationary controlled system. Travel, route editing, split/merge, allied support, hostile arrival and combat remain separate delivery work. There are no unavailable travel or attack buttons. The image implies no new ship class or ability. The production component, symbol and reducer supply gallery specimens and lab replay, including pending, reconnect, accepted and formed states with equal reduced-motion outcomes.

## Reservations, settlement and privacy

`fleet.form` validates exactly its kind, revision, system and integer fighter/infantry counts. At least one fighter is required; every fighter carries at most ten infantry. Formation requires orbital control, and embarking infantry requires unified control. Current fleets plus accepted formation reservations count toward the 128-fleet empire limit. Formation reservations make local units unavailable for another order, but leave their physical counts unchanged until settlement. Each order keeps its server sequence, identity and first eligible future tick.

`fleet.disband` targets one owned stationed fleet and rejects duplicate pending disband orders. Both commands use the existing encrypted ingress, development authorization, revision transaction and exact acknowledgement binding. Settlement revalidates control and forces, applies due commands in admitted order and releases a failed reservation without duplicating/refunding physical units. Forming deducts local forces and creates one manifest; disbanding deletes it and returns its units exactly once. Units never leave the empire when preparing a fleet.

Production caps count all local and fleet fighters/carried infantry after settlement. Stellar reports count local and stationed-fleet forces together, so moving units into a manifest cannot hide them from an acquired stellar observation. Reservations remain physical local forces and are already counted. Own fleet identities/manifests, orders and outcomes appear only in the owner's encrypted projection/history. Foreign observers receive permitted aggregate stellar facts, not private fleet IDs or command plans. Individual hostile-fleet reports, moving sensors and last-observed travel positions are not implemented here.

The existing canonical storage reservation includes the new state before admission; capacity rejection preserves accepted work. Normal orders retain the 600 KB reserved canonical bound. Disbanding alone can use a 700 KB bound, still below the encrypted document limit, so a full persistent manifest cannot block the action that releases its capacity. Its settlement replaces orders with smaller outcomes and removes successful fleets. A later tick clears outcomes. This is an additional measured operational bound, not permission to discard fleets or orders. Production schema-2 campaigns with absent military data have no fleets; only a new explicit command creates one. Schema-1 campaigns remain without production or formation. No historical fleet state is invented.
